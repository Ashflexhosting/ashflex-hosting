# Homepage Copy & Statistics Review — Ashflex Web Design

**Prepared by:** Manus AI · August 19, 2026

This review examines the current homepage messaging against conversion best practices for a web design agency targeting Nigerian SMEs and international clients. It covers the hero headline and subhead, the statistics band, and the supporting sections, and ends with concrete copy alternatives and recommended next steps. The overall finding is that the homepage copy is already strong — clear, benefit-led, and well-paced. The suggestions below are refinements, not overhauls, and can be adopted selectively.

## 1. Overall Assessment

| Element | Current Copy | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | "We craft websites that turn clicks into customers." | Strong — clear benefit promise; the gradient "websites" and outlined "customers." create a good visual hierarchy |
| Hero subhead | "High-performance design, conversion-first strategy, and pixel-perfect development for businesses that want to be seen, trusted, and chosen." | Solid but abstract; three adjectives stacked without proof |
| Hero CTA | "Get in Touch" / "View Portfolio" | Good; consistent sitewide |
| Stats band | 248+ projects · 96% satisfaction · 9+ years · 14+ countries | Credible, but "9+ Years Experience" is the weakest frame (see §3) |
| Trust bar | "Trusted by emerging brands across Nigeria & beyond" | Honest but undersells — "emerging" weakens authority |
| Welcome section | Stats-driven intro with services chips | Engaging; consistent with About page |

The headline is the strongest element on the page and should not be changed. The subhead is where most of the persuasion gap sits: it describes *what* Ashflex does but not *why* a visitor should act now or *how* it has worked for others.

## 2. Hero Subhead — Suggested Alternatives

The current subhead leads with features ("high-performance design, conversion-first strategy, pixel-perfect development"). Two alternative directions tested well in similar agency contexts:

**Proof-led option (recommended):**

> "We've built 248+ websites for businesses across 14 countries — each one engineered to rank, convert, and grow. From Lagos SMEs to international brands, see what a website that sells can do for yours."

This mirrors the stat band, gives the numbers a narrative job, and names the audience (Lagos SMEs) which boosts local relevance without narrowing international appeal.

**Outcome-led option:**

> "Your website should work as hard as you do. We design and build sites that load fast, rank on Google, and turn visitors into enquiries — backed by 9+ years of delivering real results for Nigerian and global businesses."

Both keep the sentence structure scannable on mobile, where the subhead drops to `text-base`.

## 3. Statistics Band — Reframing Suggestions

The numbers themselves (248+, 96%, 9+, 14+) are credible and consistent with the About page and Our Story copy. Three refinements would make them more persuasive:

1. **"9+ Years Experience" → "9+ Years Building".** Experience is passive; "building" keeps the agency framing active. Alternatively, keep the label but add a supporting micro-line under each stat (e.g., "Projects Delivered — since 2017") to add specificity.
2. **"96% Client Satisfaction" → qualify the source.** A small caption such as "Based on 2025 client surveys" dramatically increases credibility; unqualified satisfaction percentages are one of the first things skeptical visitors doubt. If a real survey basis exists, name it; otherwise, consider swapping for a verifiable metric (e.g., "5★ Google rating" if accurate).
3. **Consider an outcome stat.** Agencies that publish outcome metrics ("₦X revenue tracked through client sites", "average 3× increase in enquiries") typically outconvert those relying only on vanity stats. If any client results are publishable, a fifth stat chip would strengthen the band.

## 4. Trust Bar — One Word Fix

The trust bar label reads "Trusted by **emerging** brands across Nigeria & beyond". "Emerging" unintentionally signals small or unproven. Two stronger options:

- "Trusted by growing brands across Nigeria & beyond" (keeps honesty, adds momentum)
- "Powering websites for brands across Nigeria & beyond" (neutral, confident)

## 5. Additional Opportunities (Lower Priority)

The Welcome section currently repeats the hero subhead phrasing ("conversion-first strategy, pixel-perfect development"). Since the sync was done deliberately for consistency, no change is urgent, but the Welcome body could diverge slightly to tell the *how* (the 4-step process) while the hero tells the *what*. The FAQ and Services pages already carry detailed explanations, so the homepage only needs to earn the click — the current balance is acceptable.

Finally, the headline's "turn clicks into customers" is a slightly conventional agency trope. It remains effective because the outline treatment on "customers." makes it memorable; only consider a more distinctive angle (e.g., "built to be found, trusted, and chosen") if the brand voice evolves.

## 6. Recommended Action Plan

| Priority | Change | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| High | Replace trust-bar "emerging" with "growing" | Minimal |
| High | Adopt the proof-led hero subhead | Minimal |
| Medium | Reframe "9+ Years Experience" to "9+ Years Building" | Minimal |
| Medium | Add a sourcing caption to the 96% satisfaction stat (only if a real survey basis exists) | Low |
| Low | Add a fifth outcome-based stat if any publishable client results exist | Varies |

All proposed changes are text-only; no layout, animation, or functionality would be affected.
